The model

Three parts work together: a search that decides each buy by playing the game forward hundreds of times, a small neural network that tells the search which buys look promising and how good a position is, and the GA genome from the earlier evolution project, which plays everything that is not a buy (which action to play, what to trash with Chapel, what to gain with Workshop…). Training makes the network learn from the search, so the next search starts from better guesses.

Why Dominion needs a special kind of search

In chess, a position is a position. In Dominion, at the moment you buy you don't know the order of your own draw pile or what is in the opponent's hand. Two classic answers:

  • Chance nodes: one branch per possible draw. A 5-card hand from a 15-card deck already has thousands of outcomes, so the tree explodes.
  • Determinization, used here: before each simulation, deal the hidden cards anew at random (the engine's de_hide: shuffle my draw pile; put the opponent's hand back into their deck, shuffle, deal them a new hand). The tree's nodes stand for what I can know (the sequence of buys so far), not for the exact cards, so every simulation lands in the same tree and their results add up. Averaged over 400 deals, the value of a buy is its expected value over the luck of the draw. This is information-set MCTS (IS-MCTS).

All the decks' contents are known in Dominion (every buy is public), only the order is hidden, which is the case where determinization works best.

The network

A plain multi-layer perceptron, small enough to run inside the C search 400 times per buy:

layersizewhat it is
input175what the buyer knows (see How it sees)
hidden 1128, ReLU
hidden 2128, ReLU
policy head34 logitsone per card (33) + "buy nothing"; masked to the buys that are possible
value head1, tanhexpected result for the buyer: +1 win, 0 shared, −1 loss

43,555 weights. Trained in numpy (no GPU on this machine) with Adam; the forward pass in C is checked against numpy by a test.

How it learns

  1. Warm start (iteration 0). 12,000 games of the GA genome (against itself and the three bots) give 408,000 buy decisions. The net learns to copy the GA's buy (it ends up agreeing 98% of the time) and to predict who wins. Starting from zero is the expensive part of AlphaZero; the GA lets the run skip most of it.
  2. Self-play. 1,000 games per iteration on random kingdoms: 70% search against search, 30% search against the GA base genome. To explore, the first 6 turns' buys are drawn in proportion to the visit counts instead of taking the most-visited one.
  3. Training targets (expert iteration). For every searched buy, the policy learns the buy the search chose (the most-visited one), and the value learns the game's final result. Replay buffer: the last 5 iterations. AlphaZero's own target, the visit shares, failed here (run r1): see below.
  4. Evaluation after every iteration, on the same 800 games each time: search and the bare net against Big Money, Smithy-Big Money, Witch-Big Money and the GA base genome. Plus, now and then, a goal check on 1,600 games that are never used for anything else.

Why the leaf value is half rollout

AlphaZero scores leaves with the value network alone. Here that failed. After the warm start the value net's error is still large (Dominion's outcome between two similar players is close to a coin flip until late in the game), and the search trusts its noise: with the net's value alone, more simulations made it worse (48% against the GA base at 1,600 simulations). Finishing each leaf's game with the GA genome is unbiased but noisy; the mix of the two won clearly (58% at 400 simulations, 60% at 1,600). This is AlphaGo's original recipe (value net + fast rollouts). Games in this engine take 0.2 ms, so rollouts are affordable. As the value net improves, the mix can move towards the net. The measurements are in the goal table on the Log.

Why the target is the chosen buy, not the visit shares

In run r1 the net learned the search's visit shares, as in AlphaZero. In Dominion the values of the candidate buys are usually within a few percent of each other, so after 400 simulations the visits are spread mostly by the net's own prior and by the Dirichlet noise added at the root for exploration. The net learned that noise: alone, it fell from 51.7% to 24.3% against the GA base after one iteration. The search's decision (the most-visited buy) is what measurably beats the GA (58%), so r2 teaches exactly that, without root noise. A third option, Gumbel AlphaZero's "completed Q" target, was tried in between: it is noise-free, but with the warm net's very sharp prior it hardly moves off the prior, so the net would learn nothing new.

What the GA genome still does

Every decision that is not a buy: which action to play next, Chapel/Sentry trashing, Workshop/Remodel/Artisan gains, Militia discards, Library skips… It is the specialist of dominion-v1's portfolio-v2 best suited to the kingdom (ranked by its score against the bots, among those whose key cards are dealt). The comparison against the "GA base" is therefore fair: both sides play their action cards the same way, and only the buys differ.
